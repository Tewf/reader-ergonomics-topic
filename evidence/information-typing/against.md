# Against: null results, critiques and failed fits

This file gathers the evidence against typed units: null results, a failed replication, critiques of type grammars, and the costs practice reports.

## Null results for the typed format itself

- Horn 1969: the same content with or without the typed format gave adjusted posttest means of 19.56 and 17.65, not a significant difference (t = 1.17, 38 df).
  - The authors give two readings: the visible features matter less than the content analysis, or the classroom test was too coarse.
  - Students with the typed version reported more study time (5.21 against 3.87 hours), also not significant.
- Kools 2008: neither the number of headings nor the Information Mapping order changed search speed; only having headings did (abstract).
- Both tests are small and old; neither was replicated in this search.

## A genre effect that did not replicate

- Zwaan 1994 found that framing one text as literature or as news changed reading time and memory (secondary).
- Triantafyllopoulos 2021 ran three online experiments and found no difference in comprehension by genre expectation.
- Its authors conclude that fiction and nonfiction do not exert universal effects, especially in digital reading.
- So a type label read by the reader may not change reading at all.

## Effects that fade

- Text structure instruction lost its advantage at the delayed posttest (Bogaerds-Hazenberg 2020).
- Far transfer was small and inconsistent (Hebert 2016).
- Pyle 2017 found larger effects on researcher-made measures and with researcher implementers (abstract); such measures often inflate effects (an inference, not tested there).

## Type grammars as theory

- Black 1979: of the story grammars, one was formally adequate; they missed many stories and accepted a class of non-stories.
- The same paper argues the grammars add nothing to models of story content.
- Trabasso 1985 moved the field to causal networks of events, a content account (title and venue only).
- Lesson for the topic: a type can be checked by a program, but passing the check does not make a unit good of its kind.

## Which list of types

- Horn 1969 lists eight map types; a 2018 answer by the author of Baker 2013 lists six, "originally seven" (WSE 2018a). Information Mapping 2026 (example only) lists none on its method page.
- DITA's own specification says many DITA types are not closely connected with Information Mapping (OASIS 2015, 2.2.1.4).
- The canonical set has changed over time, so "the" information types are a convention, not a finding.

## Rigidity and authoring cost

- DITA forbids nested sections and untitled topics; a type that needs them is no longer DITA (OASIS 2015, B.11).
- S1000D allows only added constraints on a fixed schema, where DITA allows derived types (WSE 2018b).
- Deriving a type needs a developer, says one asker; the answer calls a simple derivation trivially easy (SO 2013a).
- Tool and set-up costs fall on small teams (WSE 2014a).
- Writers' motivation fell when component content management was framed only as efficiency (Batova 2018).
- One topic per task fragments a help system into many tiny pages; the critic proposes grouping related tasks (Johnson 2013).
- A story template forced on content harms the story, says a practitioner (WSE 2020a, example only).

## Typing for language models

- No vendor claim on typed content and model accuracy was read in full; one page timed out and is not cited.
- Mortezaagha 2026 shows the trade-off: structure-aware retrieval covers more sections, while plain content similarity ranks the single best chunk higher (abstract, secondary).

## Limits of the evidence used here

- Most formal sources were read by abstract only; the CCM reviews by title only (sources.md).
- Information Mapping 2026 (5 of 20) and Baker 2018b (9.5 of 20) are below the threshold and are examples only.
- Company and committee pages describe their own method; DITAWriter 2026 is an informal list by one practitioner.
- Q/A threads are opinion, with no data.
- Minimalism (Carroll 1987; Lazonder 1993), the lineage of the task type, was not read; its results are not used.
